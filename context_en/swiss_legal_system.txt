=== THE SWISS LEGAL SYSTEM (For LLM Agents) ===

This document describes how the Swiss legal system is structured,
so the agent can navigate like an attorney — not just search semantically.

--- HIERARCHY OF LEGAL SOURCES ---

Switzerland has a STRICT hierarchy of legal sources:

1. Federal Constitution (BV) — Supreme Law
   → Fundamental rights (Art. 7-36 BV)
   → State organization (Art. 143-191 BV)
   → EVERY federal statute must conform to the BV
   → Art. 190 BV: Federal statutes are binding on the Federal Court (even if unconstitutional!)

2. Federal Statutes (laws in the formal sense)
   → Enacted by Parliament
   → Systematic Collection (SR number)
   → Examples: OR (SR 220), ZGB (SR 210), StGB (SR 311.0), StPO (SR 312.0)

3. Ordinances (Federal Council ordinances)
   → Concretize statutes
   → Issued by Federal Council (executive)
   → e.g.: AVIG → AVIV (Unemployment Insurance Ordinance)

4. Federal Court Practice (BGE + unpublished decisions)
   → Interprets statutes and BV
   → BGE = Leading decisions (published, authoritative)
   → Unpublished decisions = individual case application

--- HOW THE FEDERAL COURT WORKS ---

The Swiss Federal Court (BGer) in Lausanne is the LAST instance.

Path to the Federal Court (chain of instances):
  First instance (District Court / Bezirksgericht)
  → Second instance (Cantonal Court / Obergericht)
  → Federal Court (last instance, legal questions only)

Types of appeals to the Federal Court (Art. 72-89 BGG):
  • Appeal in civil matters (Art. 72 BGG) — amount in dispute ≥ CHF 30,000
  • Appeal in criminal matters (Art. 78 BGG) — criminal judgments + detention issues
  • Appeal in public law matters (Art. 82 BGG) — administrative, tax, immigration law
  • Subsidiary constitutional complaint (Art. 113 BGG) — when no other appeal is available

CRITICAL: In EVERY Federal Court case, the following provisions are examined:
  → Standing/legitimation (Art. 76/81/89 BGG)
  → Appeal deadline (Art. 100 BGG — 30 days)
  → Grounds for complaint (Art. 95 BGG — violation of law)
  → Binding factual findings (Art. 105 BGG)
  → Reasoning requirements (Art. 42 para. 2 BGG)

--- DIVISIONS OF THE FEDERAL COURT ---

5 divisions with clear jurisdictional allocation:

Division I — Public Law (Lausanne)
   → Criminal procedural coercive measures (detention! → prefix 1B_)
   → Administrative law, spatial planning (→ prefix 1C_)
   → BGE publication: BGE [volume] I [page]

Division II — Public Law (Lausanne)
   → Immigration law, tax law (→ prefix 2C_)
   → BGE publication: BGE [volume] II [page]

Division I — Civil Law (Lausanne)
   → Contract law, liability, employment law (→ prefix 4A_)
   → BGE publication: BGE [volume] III [page]

Division II — Civil Law (Lausanne)
   → Family law, succession law, property law (→ prefix 5A_)
   → BGE publication: BGE [volume] III [page]

Criminal Law Division (Lausanne)
   → Criminal judgments, sentencing (→ prefix 6B_)
   → BGE publication: BGE [volume] IV [page]

Social Insurance Division (Lucerne)
   → Sub-division 1: IV, UV, ALV (→ prefix 8C_)
   → Sub-division 2: AHV, KV, BVG, EL (→ prefix 9C_)
   → BGE publication: BGE [volume] V [page]

--- CROSS-REFERENCES: HOW STATUTES ARE INTERCONNECTED ---

CRITICAL: Swiss statutes are HEAVILY cross-referenced. A legal question
ALWAYS involves multiple statutes simultaneously.

Typical linking patterns:

1. CONTRACT: OR (substance) + ZGB Art. 2 (good faith) + BGG (procedure)
2. DIVORCE: ZGB (substance) + ZPO (procedure) + BGG (appeal) + BV Art. 29
3. CRIMINAL PROCEEDINGS: StGB (offense) + StPO (procedure) + BGG + BV Art. 29-32
4. DETENTION: StPO Art. 212-240 (substance) + BV Art. 10/31 (freedom) + BGG (appeal)
5. SOCIAL INSURANCE: IVG/KVG/BVG (specific) + ATSG (general part) + BGG + BV
6. IMMIGRATION LAW: AIG (substance) + BV Art. 13 (private life) + ECHR Art. 8 + BGG
7. TAX: DBG/StHG (substance) + BV Art. 127 (taxation principles) + BGG

Rule: EVERY legal question has at least:
  (a) Substantive law (substance statute)
  (b) Procedural law (ZPO/StPO/VwVG)
  (c) Appellate law (BGG if Federal Court)
  (d) Constitutional law (BV — fundamental rights affected)

--- ERWÄGUNGEN (E.) — THE HEART OF CASE LAW ---

Every Federal Court decision is structured in numbered considerations (Erwägungen / E.):

Typical structure:
  E. 1 — Procedural requirements (standing, deadline, amount in dispute)
  E. 2 — Facts and complaints
  E. 3 — Legal assessment (CORE consideration)
  E. 3.1 — Sub-consideration (specific legal question)
  E. 3.2 — Further sub-consideration
  E. 4 — Result / cost allocation

EVERY consideration is a SEPARATE document in the corpus!
  → "BGE 137 IV 122 E. 6.2" = one specific consideration
  → "1B_210/2023 E. 4.1" = one specific consideration

For search this means:
  → Search for the CONTENT of the consideration
  → The same legal question can be discussed in E. 3 OR E. 4 OR E. 2.3
  → NEVER search by E. number — always by the legal concept

--- BGE NUMBERING ---

Format: BGE [volume] [division-roman] [page] E. [consideration]

Examples:
  BGE 137 IV 122 E. 6.2
  │    │   │   │   └── Consideration 6.2
  │    │   │   └── Page 122
  │    │   └── Division IV (Criminal Law)
  │    └── Volume 137
  └── Federal Court Decision (leading case)

Division mapping:
  I = Public Law (Division 1)
  II = Public Law (Division 2) — Immigration, Tax
  III = Civil Law (both divisions)
  IV = Criminal Law
  V = Social Insurance Law

Unpublished decisions:
  Format: [prefix]_[number]/[year] E. [consideration]
  Example: 1B_210/2023 E. 4.1
  Example: 5A_800/2019 E. 2.

--- SUBSUMTION: THE LEGAL REASONING PROCESS ---

Subsumtion = "Does the factual situation fit under the legal provision?"

Major premise: The provision (e.g. Art. 41 OR: "Anyone who unlawfully causes damage to another...")
Minor premise: The facts (e.g. "Defendant ran a red light and injured plaintiff")
Conclusion: The legal consequence (e.g. "→ obligation to pay damages")

For SEARCH this means:
  → Search for the ELEMENTS of the legal provision
  → Not the facts, but the LEGAL CONCEPT
  → "Unlawfulness", "damage", "causation", "fault"
  → NOT: "car accident", "injury", "hospital"

--- GENERAL PARTS (always applicable) ---

Many statutes have a "General Part" that ALWAYS applies:

• OR Art. 1-183: General Part of the Law of Obligations
  → Applies to EVERY contract (sale, construction, lease, employment...)
  → Art. 1-10: Formation of contract
  → Art. 11-16: Form requirements
  → Art. 17-40: Defects of consent, agency
  → Art. 41-61: Tort liability (non-contractual)
  → Art. 62-67: Unjust enrichment
  → Art. 68-96: Performance
  → Art. 97-109: Non-performance

• ATSG Art. 1-78: General Part of Social Insurance
  → Applies equally to IVG, KVG, BVG, AVIG, UVG, AHV
  → Art. 16-21: Benefits general
  → Art. 25: Restitution
  → Art. 43-49: Administrative procedure
  → Art. 56-62: Legal remedies

• ZGB Art. 1-10: Introductory articles
  → Apply to ALL private law (ZGB + OR)
  → Art. 2: Good faith (cited in almost EVERY civil case)
  → Art. 4: Judicial discretion
  → Art. 8: Burden of proof (appears in 30%+ of civil cases)
